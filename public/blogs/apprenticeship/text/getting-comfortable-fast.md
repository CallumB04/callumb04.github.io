# Getting comfortable, fast

Being in the situation I was, with responsibilities and expectations building up fast, it was necessary that I got comfortable quickly.

Whilst being the only developer was a steep learning curve originally, this quickly became an obvious upside. Every decision made was mine, and so was every mistake, which allowed me to quickly learn from them each time.

Early into my role I published a bug to the live site. This was my first real mistake, and a lesson I learnt quickly. After this, I saw the true importance of rigorous testing and having a strict development process, and designed and implemented a new system for all development in the company.

## What actually helped

There were a few things that helped me early on and I continue to do even today, which allowed me to get very comfortable in my role, and have a strong process as a developer.

1. Frequent communication with my manager and heads of other departments. This helped me understand the business quickly, stay aligned to the company goals and what the team needed, and ensure my efforts were going into the right place.
2. Effective project management. What originally started on Notion, and eventually shifted to Linear. I built an efficient system for managing our development roadmap, and agile processes for all projects. This helped my productivity stay consistent, and provided a clear product roadmap for the wider team. This also helped me collaborate effectively with other functions in the business.
3. Experimentation, but safely. I built a development environment with realistic data, but no risks to live clients. This allowed me to experiment and learn the technologies I was using, and build a strong testing process to prevent any issues shipping on the live site.

Alongside all of this, in the early months of my full-time role, I also began the coursework for my apprenticeship. This accounted for 20% of my work hours, involving:

- Completing online courses
- Project writeups
- Attending live events, sometimes over a week away from my job role

These off-the-job hours created some challenges, but I balanced them around my role effectively to ensure no projects were left half-finished when I had to focus on apprenticeship work. This was also incredibly important to manage well, being the only software developer in the business.
