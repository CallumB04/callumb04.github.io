# What I built

Following the initial 3 months at the company, my responsibilities continued to grow, and therefore I have had the opportunity to work on some really impactful and interesting projects over the past 18 months. This includes a variety of additions to our primary user platform, LeadersLab, but also internal tooling to automate processes for the sales and delivery teams, and improve our offering as a business.

## Low-code

LeadersLab is our primary web platform, currently used by around 7,000 users, across 35 active client programmes. I began working on it from the start, using low-code technologies: [Bubble.io](https://bubble.io) and [n8n](https://n8n.io). This was a big change for me, coming from building web applications in React and other coding frameworks before my apprenticeship, to now working in a totally new environment. However, once comfortable, this platform allowed me to implement features at a much faster rate, and the ability to effectively maintain a large scale application, with little professional experience.

Throughout the past 18 months, I have continued to improve and add new features to LeadersLab. Some examples include:

- **Tensai**, the AI leadership coach I mentioned earlier. The initial version of this was just a chat bot that was passed some details of the user within the prompt to personalise it. However, eventually this transformed into a truly bespoke experience, using a vector database to store and securely pull information about that user, their company, and the 10X Leadership and Management knowledge that helped shape Tensai's answers and create high-quality responses. Tensai also gained many different conversation types, such as a debrief of a user's Strength Index Assessment (a key product and assessment tool on LeadersLab), and a premium voice chat mode, that used ElevenLabs AI voice functionalities to hold high quality, live voice conversations with the user. This collection of refinements and new functionality to Tensai, allowed it to stand out as our primary product on the platform - around 30% of users on an active programme now use it monthly - driving revenue, and significantly improving a user's development capability on LeadersLab.
- **Execution plans**. An iterative journey, supported by Tensai, where users build sprints of LeadersLab resources, exercises and reflections, with the goal of achieving a final action, or solving a challenge. This became another key product for the business, and heavily supported the development journey of our users in programmes, by allowing them to build Execution Plans in between their workshops, and _execute_ on the knowledge they learnt from the sessions.
- **Automated Masterclass builder**, turning podcast-style interviews into publishable content with short impactful insights, and their respective video clips. It used AI to analyse the interviews, and automated the full process from pasting a Vimeo link, to the content being ready to publish. This saved large amounts of time for our delivery team, taking a full day of work down to a 30 minute process that runs in the background, and only needs lightly checking before it is published. I also built other similar automations, such as an AI assisted course builder, which saved further time and effort when pushing content onto LeadersLab.

## Beyond low-code

Towards the end of my apprenticeship, in the 3-6 months before writing this, I started to slowly implement full-code solutions into our business. Initially this began as internal tools to assist the sales and delivery teams in their process, however has eventually turned into a full scale rebuild of LeadersLab.

Building LeadersLab early on, I immediately noticed the bottlenecks and issues with the technologies we were using. Whilst Bubble and n8n allowed for quick fixes and implementations of features, they were heavily restrictive in terms of what we could build, testing processes that we could implement, and the speed of the application for users. It became a ticking time bomb that eventually we knew needed to be replaced as we continued to scale, and in recent months I finally began rebuilding LeadersLab from the ground up.

For this rebuild, I decided to use React for the frontend due to my existing experience with it, Go for the backend due to simple setup, strong ecosystem and built-in libraries, and fast speeds, and Supabase for auth and database due to reliability and easy prototyping. This also gave me the ability to build a proper development process around it, with tests running on every pull request, and a failing run stopping it from being merged, which was never possible on Bubble. Every feature has now been migrated across, as well as new additions that the old stack made impractical, such as gamification to drive higher platform retention. Throughout developing the rebuild, it's clear the migration will be a game changer for our platform, not only in terms of user experience, but also allowing for a huge streamline in development capabilities, especially with the rise in productivity possibility with AI.

Another example of full-code implementations were the tools I built for delivery. I built multiple repositories, with a combination of preset code snippets, and detailed Claude and skill files, to allow for non-technical people in the business to build workshops, reports and more, within Claude Code. Over 100 workshops have been built this way so far, taking what was 1-2 days of work across multiple people, down to a couple of hours for a single performance partner, by replacing a lot of the previous manual work with AI-assisted equivalents, whilst minimising risk or issues with the generated code, due to my preset snippets, and lots of strict guidelines within the markdown documentation.
